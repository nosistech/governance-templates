## 1. Purpose

[Company Name] uses artificial intelligence (AI) tools to work faster and to serve our [customers / clients] better. This policy tells you how to use AI tools safely at work. It protects our data, our [customers'] data, and our reputation.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 Who this policy applies to
This policy applies to all employees, contractors, interns, and temporary staff. Vendors that use AI to do work for us must follow the AI terms in their contracts.

### 2.2 Which tools this policy applies to
This policy applies to every AI tool that you use for company work, on a company device or a personal device. This includes:

- chat assistants, for example ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot
- AI features inside software that we already use, for example email, documents, CRM, and design tools
- meeting recorders and AI notetakers
- browser extensions and AI browsers
- code assistants
- AI agents that can take actions, and AI that connects to our systems

### 2.3 Definitions
- **AI tool:** software that generates text, images, audio, video, code, or decisions from an instruction (a "prompt").
- **Approved tool:** an AI tool that has the status "Approved" or "Approved with conditions" in our AI Tool Register.
- **AI agent:** an AI tool that can take actions, for example send messages, change files, or use other software.
- **Output:** anything that an AI tool produces.
- **Personal account:** an AI account that you created yourself, including free accounts, and not one that [Company Name] provides.

## 3. Roles

| Role | Who | Responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| Policy owner | [Name, title] | Keeps this policy current, answers questions, approves exceptions |
| Tool approver | [Name, title, or AI review group] | Decides on requests for new tools and new uses |
| Tool owner | One named person for each approved tool | Sets up the tool, controls access, checks that it works as expected |
| Managers | All people managers | Make sure that their teams know and follow this policy |
| All staff | Everyone in scope | Follow this policy and report problems |

## 4. Approved tools and new requests

1. Use only approved tools for company work. The list of approved tools is in the AI Tool Register: [link].
2. Use the company account for each tool. Do not use personal accounts for company work. Personal accounts often let the vendor keep your data or train its AI on it.
3. To request a new tool or a new use of an approved tool, send [the request form / an email] to [tool approver]. Give the tool name, the task, and the type of data that the tool will see.
4. We decide on requests within [5] business days. Low-risk requests can be faster.
5. Before we approve a tool, we review the vendor. We check how the vendor uses, stores, and protects our data, and what the contract says.
6. A new AI feature inside software that we already use counts as a new tool. Do not turn it on until we approve it.

## 5. Data rules

Before you give any data to an AI tool, find the data tier in this table. Then use only the tools allowed for that tier.

| Tier | Examples | Tools you can use |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Information that we already published, for example our website, press releases, and public reports | Any approved tool |
| **Internal** | Internal emails, drafts, meeting notes, plans, and general business documents | Approved tools that have a business contract that does not let the vendor train its AI on our data |
| **Restricted** | Personal data about customers or staff, [client confidential information], financial account data, unreleased financial results, source code that is under a non-disclosure agreement, passwords, API keys, and other secrets | Only tools that the AI Tool Register approves for Restricted data. Passwords, API keys, and other secrets: never, in any AI tool |

Also follow these rules:

- If you do not know the tier, treat the data as Restricted.
- Remove names and other identifying details if the task does not need them.
- Give the AI tool only the part of a document that the task needs.

## 6. Allowed and prohibited uses

### 6.1 Allowed uses
You can use approved tools, with data of the correct tier, to:

- draft emails, documents, and presentations for you to review
- summarize documents and meetings
- brainstorm ideas and plan work
- research topics, if you check the sources yourself
- write and review code, if the code goes through our normal review and tests
- translate text, if a person checks important translations
- analyze data, if the AI Tool Register approves the tool for that data tier

### 6.2 Prohibited uses
Do not use any AI tool to:

- make a final decision about a person, for example hiring, firing, discipline, pay, or credit, without review and approval by a qualified person
- create content that is false, misleading, discriminatory, or harassing
- copy the voice, face, or writing of a real person without their permission
- record or transcribe a meeting or call without the consent of all participants
- avoid security controls, or monitor other people without authorization
- give Restricted data to a tool that is not approved for it
- go against a law, a contract, or a client instruction

## 7. Check the output

1. You are responsible for all work that you deliver, including work that AI helped to make.
2. AI output can be wrong or incomplete. AI tools can also invent facts, quotes, and sources.
3. Before you use AI output, check all facts, numbers, names, and dates.
4. Open and read every source or citation that an AI tool gives you. Do not use a source that you cannot find.
5. Review and test AI-generated code like all other code.
6. Get a second person to review AI-assisted work before it goes to [customers, regulators, or the public]. Do the same before you use it for legal or financial decisions.
7. AI must not make the final decision about a person. A qualified person must make that decision and be able to explain it.

## 8. Transparency

1. If customers talk to an AI system, for example a chatbot or an AI phone agent, tell them that it is AI. Also tell them how to reach a person.
2. Follow all laws that require AI disclosure in our industry and locations. [List the laws that apply, for example state laws on AI in health care or hiring.]
3. If a contract or a client asks you to disclose AI use, do so.
4. If someone asks whether you used AI for a piece of work, answer honestly.

## 9. Agents, connections, and code

1. Do not connect an AI tool to company systems without approval from [IT / the tool owner]. Company systems include email, file storage, CRM, code repositories, and payment systems.
2. Give each AI agent the minimum access that it needs. Use read-only access when possible.
3. A person must approve each agent action that is difficult to reverse. Examples: sending external messages, making payments, deleting data, changing live systems, and changing access rights.
4. Each AI agent has a named owner who monitors it and can stop it immediately.
5. Keep a log of the actions that each agent takes.
6. Do not install AI browser extensions or AI browsers without approval. They can read everything that is on your screen.
7. AI tools can follow hidden instructions in websites, emails, and documents ("prompt injection"). Do not let an AI tool act on content from outside the company without a person checking the result.

## 10. Training

1. Complete the AI training [before you use AI tools / within 30 days after you join].
2. Complete a refresher course [every 12 months].
3. The training covers this policy, the data tiers, how to check output, and AI risks, for example prompt injection and deepfakes.
4. [Company Name] keeps a record of who completed the training.

## 11. Report problems

Tell [contact, email, or channel] immediately if:

- Restricted data went into a tool that is not approved for it
- AI output caused harm, or almost caused harm
- an AI tool or agent did something unexpected or unsafe
- you received content that could be a deepfake, for example an unusual voice message from a manager that asks for a payment

Report even if you are not sure. We do not punish good-faith reports of mistakes. The [AI incident-response playbook] explains what happens next.

## 12. Exceptions, enforcement, and review

1. **Exceptions:** Ask the policy owner in writing. Give the reason, the time limit, and the extra controls that you will use. The policy owner records all exceptions.
2. **Enforcement:** If you do not follow this policy, you can lose access to AI tools. Disciplinary action is also possible, as our other policies and the law allow.
3. **Your rights:** This policy does not limit your rights under the law, including your right to discuss the terms and conditions of your employment.
4. **Monitoring:** [Company Name] can keep logs of company AI tool use for [90] days. We use the logs to protect data and to meet legal duties, as the law allows.
5. **Review:** The policy owner reviews this policy at least every 12 months. The policy owner also reviews it when:
   - a new law applies to us
   - we start a new high-risk use of AI
   - a serious AI incident occurs
   - a main AI vendor changes how it uses our data
